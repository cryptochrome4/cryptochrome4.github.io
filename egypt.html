Hieroglyph







Description

  • A (Vulture): This glyph actually represents a glottal sound, not the vowel "A" we use today.
  • B (Leg): It creates the "b" sound and was often used in words related to walking or movement.
  • C (Cup): This basket symbol stands for a hard "K" sound, representing a container or vessel.
  • D (Hand): It represents the "d" sound and was used to write words about actions, giving, or doing.
  • E (Feather): A single reed leaf that sounds like "y" or "i." Double leaves create a long "ee" sound.
  • F (Viper): This horned snake makes the "f" sound and often appeared as a pronoun meaning "he" or "him."
  • G (Pot): This jar stand creates the hard "g" sound and was linked to storage or offerings.
  • H (Wick): A twisted flax wick making the "h" sound, often used in words about light or fire.
  • I (Feather): Similar to "E," a single reed leaf used for short vowel sounds like "i" or "y."
  • J (Cobra): This snake symbol makes the "dj" sound, famously used to write the word for "god" (Netcher).
  • K (Cup): A basket with a handle that produces the "k" sound, frequently meaning "you" (masculine).
  • L (Lion): Represented a recumbent lion for the "l" sound, a symbol of royal power and protection.
  • M (Owl): This famous owl symbol makes the "m" sound and was the standard word for "in" or "from."
  • N (Water): These ripples of water create the "n" sound, representing the flow of life or energy.
  • O (Chick): A quail chick that makes the "w" or "u" sound, often used to indicate plural words.
  • P (Stool): A reed mat or stool creating the "p" sound, forming the base of words about places or seating.
  • Q (Hill): A sandy hillside or slope that represents a sharp, emphasized "q" (or hard "k") sound.
  • R (Mouth): A human mouth symbol making the "r" sound, which also functioned as the literal word for "mouth."
  • S (Cloth): A folded piece of cloth representing the "s" sound, commonly found in words for clothing or fabric.
  • T (Loaf): A small loaf of bread making the "t" sound, which was the standard suffix to make words feminine.
  • U (Chick): Sharing the same quail chick symbol as "O," it creates the "u" or "w" vowel sound.
  • V (Viper): Uses the same horned viper symbol as "F," often representing a soft "v" or "f" sound depending on the context.
  • W (Chick): Another variation of the quail chick, essential for forming the plural and grammatical endings.
  • X (Cloth): Formed by crossing cloth or folding fabric lines, producing a sharp blended sound in later scripts.
  • Y (Feathers): Two reed leaves side-by-side that create a long "ee" or double "y" sound, meaning "my" or "me."
  • Z (Bolt): A door bolt used to lock gates, making the "z" sound and often appearing in words about security or closing.
